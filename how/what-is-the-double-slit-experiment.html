What is the double-slit experiment?

Fire tiny particles one at a time at a wall with two slits and they build up a pattern of stripes, as if each one went through both slits like a wave.

How it works

  1. Each particle lands in just one spot, like a tiny ball.
  2. After thousands of them, the spots form bright and dark stripes, which is what waves do.
  3. If you measure which slit each particle goes through, the stripes disappear.
  4. So the pattern depends on what can be known about the particle's path.

The numbers

  • First done with light1801, by Thomas Young

How do we know?

It has been done with light, with single electrons, and in 1999 even with buckyballs, molecules of 60 carbon atoms. You can fire particles yourself in the Quantum lab and turn the detectors on and off.

How do solar panels work?

Solar panels turn sunlight straight into electricity. Light knocks electrons loose inside the panel, and they flow out as a current.

How it works

  1. A panel is made of thin slices of silicon, the same element that is in sand.
  2. Sunlight arrives as tiny packets of energy called photons.
  3. When a photon hits the silicon, it can knock an electron free.
  4. The panel is built so the freed electrons all flow the same way, and that flow is electricity.

The numbers

  • Sunlight on a clear dayabout 1,000 watts on each square meter
  • Share a typical panel turns into electricityabout 20%

How do we know?

Albert Einstein explained how light knocks electrons loose in 1905 and won a Nobel Prize for it. You can watch it work by shading a solar garden light and seeing it react.

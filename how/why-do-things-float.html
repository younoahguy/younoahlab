Why do some things float and others sink?

Something floats if it is less dense than the liquid it sits in. Dense means how much stuff is packed into a certain space.

How it works

  1. When you put something in water, it pushes some water out of the way.
  2. The water pushes back with a force equal to the weight of the water that was moved.
  3. If that push is as big as the object's weight, it floats.
  4. A steel ship floats because it is shaped like a bowl full of air, so overall it is lighter than the same size block of water.

The numbers

  • Fresh water1.000 g per cubic centimeter
  • Sea waterabout 1.025 g per cubic centimeter

How do we know?

Archimedes worked this out more than 2,200 years ago. You can check it with the float tester in the Science Toolbox, or with a bowl of water and things from around the house.

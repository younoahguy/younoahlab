Why do we have seasons?

Earth is tilted. As it goes around the Sun, first one half and then the other leans toward the Sun.

How it works

  1. Earth's spin axis is tipped over by about 23.4 degrees.
  2. When your half of the world leans toward the Sun, sunlight hits it more directly and days are longer. That is summer.
  3. Six months later your half leans away. That is winter.
  4. The two halves of the world always have opposite seasons.

The numbers

  • Earth's tilt23.4°

How do we know?

It is not about distance. Earth is actually closest to the Sun in early January, in the middle of the northern winter.

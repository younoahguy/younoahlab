How does your body fight germs?

Your body has a defense team called the immune system. It spots germs, attacks them and remembers them for next time.

How it works

  1. Skin and the sticky lining of your nose keep most germs out.
  2. If germs get in, white blood cells find them and attack.
  3. Some cells make antibodies, which stick to one kind of germ and mark it for clean-up.
  4. Memory cells remember that germ, so your body can beat it much faster if it comes back. Vaccines safely teach your body the same lesson.

The numbers

  • Good hand-washing timeabout 20 seconds

How do we know?

Scientists can see white blood cells swallowing germs under a microscope, and blood tests can measure antibodies. For health questions about you, ask a grown-up or a doctor.

How do quantum computers work?

A quantum computer uses qubits, which can be in a mix of 0 and 1 and can be entangled with each other. That lets it solve certain special problems in far fewer steps.

How it works

  1. A normal computer bit is 0 or 1. A qubit can be a blend of both until it is measured.
  2. Entangled qubits act as one linked system, so the possibilities multiply very quickly.
  3. A quantum program steers the qubits so that wrong answers cancel out and right answers become more likely.
  4. Qubits are very fragile. Heat or a stray bump ruins them, so many kinds are kept colder than outer space.

The numbers

  • Many qubit chips are cooled toabout 0.015 kelvin, far colder than space

How do we know?

Quantum computers are not faster at everything, and today's machines are still experimental. Scientists check them by running problems small enough to verify on ordinary computers.

Why do planets go around the Sun?

A planet is always falling toward the Sun, but it is also moving sideways so fast that it keeps missing. That endless falling-around is an orbit.

How it works

  1. The Sun's gravity pulls every planet toward it.
  2. Each planet is also moving sideways at high speed.
  3. The two together bend its path into a loop around the Sun.
  4. Planets close to the Sun feel a stronger pull and must move faster, so their years are shorter.

The numbers

  • Earth's speed around the Sunabout 30 kilometers every second
  • Kepler's rule(year)² = (distance)³

How do we know?

Johannes Kepler found the pattern in the early 1600s from careful measurements of the planets. You can check his rule for all eight planets in the Space lab.
